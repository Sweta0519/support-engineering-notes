# The answer that built more trust than any fix

_2026-09-18 · Lessons · [Discuss on LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:share:7506633992905854976/)_

## Notes

The most trusted sentence I learned in five years of support was "I do not know yet".

Early on I thought support meant having the answer. So I stalled, dug through logs in silence, and replied when I had something certain. Customers hated the silence far more than they would have hated uncertainty.

Then a senior colleague showed me a different pattern. Reply within the hour with three things: what I can see, what I am checking next, and when they will hear from me again. Nothing else.

It felt like admitting weakness. It did the opposite. Customers stopped escalating, because they could see someone was on it. Engineers got cleaner questions, because I was writing down what I was checking. And I got faster, because saying "I do not know yet" out loud made me define exactly what I did not know.

Five years later it is still the first thing I teach anyone new to a support queue: certainty is optional, silence is not.

If your team is hiring senior support or technical support engineers for Germany remote, my inbox is open.

What is the sentence you wish you had learned earlier?
